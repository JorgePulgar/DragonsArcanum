## The Virtual Tabletop Showdown

With the rise of online play, choosing the right Virtual Tabletop (VTT) is one of the most important decisions a DM can make. Roll20 has been the industry leader for over a decade, but Foundry VTT has quickly become the favorite for those who want more control over their experience.

![Virtual Tabletop Interface](/images/roll20-vs-foundry.jpg)

### Accessibility vs. Performance

The biggest advantage of Roll20 is that it runs in a browser. You can send a link to your players, and they are in the game. Foundry VTT, while offering a much smoother and more modern interface, requires the DM to host the server themselves or pay for a hosting service.

## Automation and Customization

Foundry VTT is built from the ground up for modules and customization. If you want automatic damage calculation, complex lighting effects, or integrated soundscapes, Foundry is miles ahead. Roll20 has caught up in some areas, but its legacy code can make complex setups feel clunky.

### Marketplace and Official Support

Roll20 has a massive advantage in its marketplace. Almost every official D&D adventure is available as a pre-built module on Roll20, saving the DM hours of prep. Foundry is catching up, with official support for several systems, but it still often requires more manual work to get started.

## The Final Decision

If you are just starting out and want the easiest path to playing online, **Roll20** is the way to go. However, if you are a "power DM" who wants the best possible visual experience and isn't afraid of a bit of technical setup, **Foundry VTT** is worth every penny of its one-time purchase price.
